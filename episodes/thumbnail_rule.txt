# The Human Curriculum
## YouTube Title & Thumbnail Creative System

### 1. BRAND CORE

Channel:
The Human Curriculum

Core idea:
Learn the human skills that AI can't replace.

The channel explores:
- Emotional intelligence
- Interpersonal skills
- Self-awareness
- Communication
- Leadership
- Collaboration
- Trust
- Conflict
- Influence
- Human behavior
- Global workplace skills
- Workplace English
- Career development

Audience:
People who want to become stronger communicators, teammates, and future leaders in an AI-driven workplace.

Brand personality:
- Intelligent
- Curious
- Warm
- Modern
- Human
- Globally minded
- Ambitious but not aggressive
- Academic but accessible
- Fashionable but not superficial
- Optimistic but not motivational-speaker-like

The visual identity should feel like:

Tokyo design museum
×
Japanese editorial magazine
×
modern airport wayfinding
×
global career culture
×
illustrated learning journal
×
premium YouTube education channel

The channel should NEVER look like a generic productivity, business, or self-help channel.

2. TITLE SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Titles must be concise, intelligent, and immediately understandable.

Preferred structure:

[Concept]: [Human-readable hook]

Examples:

Emotional Granularity: The Human Skill AI Can’t Replace

Interoceptive Awareness: The 30-Second Window

Personal Triggers: The Stories Your Brain Invents

Avoid unnecessarily long titles.

Do not automatically include:
"The Human Curriculum"
"Episode 01"
"Episode 02"

unless there is a strong reason.

The channel name already establishes the series identity.

The title should prioritize:
1. The psychological / human concept
2. A memorable curiosity hook
3. Clarity
4. Searchability

Avoid clickbait such as:
- THIS WILL CHANGE YOUR LIFE
- YOU NEED TO KNOW THIS
- 5 SECRETS...
- Scientists Are Shocked...
- AI Is TERRIFIED...
- NEVER DO THIS AGAIN

The tone should feel intelligent rather than sensational.

When the episode contains a highly memorable phrase, metaphor, or question, use it as the second half of the title.

Examples:

Emotional Granularity: Name It to Tame It

Interoceptive Awareness: The 30-Second Window

Conflict: What Your Brain Gets Wrong in the Moment

Trust: Why People Decide to Follow You

Feedback: How to Hear What People Aren’t Saying

Before generating the title, identify:

- Core human skill
- Main scientific concept
- Main workplace problem
- Most memorable insight
- Most useful phrase or metaphor

Then create 3-5 candidate titles internally and select the clearest one.

Do not rank or explain the alternatives unless explicitly requested.

3. THUMBNAIL CORE RULE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every thumbnail must clearly belong to The Human Curriculum.

However:

DO NOT repeat the same composition.

The thumbnails should feel like different scenes from the same visual universe.

The viewer should recognize the channel through:

- Color system
- Illustration language
- Typography
- Character design
- Editorial composition
- Travel / global / learning motifs
- Consistent visual texture

NOT through repeating the same pose or background.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
4. VISUAL IDENTITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Maintain the following visual DNA:

Illustration:
- High-quality editorial illustration
- Hand-painted / textured digital illustration
- Sophisticated flat illustration
- Slightly imperfect painterly texture
- Contemporary Japanese editorial sensibility

Avoid:
- Generic AI art
- Corporate stock illustration
- Generic cartoon characters
- Chibi
- Anime character styling
- Overly childish educational graphics
- Photorealistic people

Character:
The main woman is a recurring visual protagonist.

Her face should be stylized and simplified.

Do NOT attempt photorealistic facial reproduction.

Keep:
- Similar overall character identity
- Similar hair identity
- Similar age impression
- Similar elegant, intelligent personality

But vary:
- Hairstyle details
- Clothing
- Accessories
- Expression
- Body orientation
- Pose
- Activity

She should feel like the same protagonist appearing in different chapters of a visual story.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
5. COLOR SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Maintain a recognizable palette across the series:

Primary:
- Navy
- Sky blue
- Cream
- Soft white

Secondary:
- Coral
- Warm red
- Dusty pink
- Mustard / warm yellow

Occasional accents:
- Green
- Burgundy

Do not use every color in every thumbnail.

Each episode should have a slightly different dominant color balance.

The overall series should remain recognizable when thumbnails are viewed together.

6. COMPOSITION VARIATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

IMPORTANT:

Never use the same composition for consecutive episodes.

Rotate between radically different compositions.

Possible compositions:

A. Character centered
Character faces the viewer.

B. Character walking
Character moves through an illustrated environment.

C. Character sitting
Character studies, reflects, reads, or observes.

D. Character leaning
Character physically interacts with a large object.

E. Character looking upward
Large concept or typography dominates the frame.

F. Character from behind
Viewer sees the character entering a new environment.

G. Character in profile
Character observes something happening outside the frame.

H. Character small / environment huge
The world dominates the composition.

I. Close-up
Face and upper body dominate the image.

J. Wide scene
Character occupies only a small portion of the frame.

K. Diagonal composition
Strong visual movement from corner to corner.

L. Split scene
Two environments or two emotional states appear in one composition.

M. Overhead view
Character and objects are viewed from above.

N. Door / window composition
Character appears through an architectural opening.

O. Transit composition
Character moves through an airport, station, map, or route system.

P. Object interaction
Character touches, carries, moves, opens, or leans on a physical object.

Q. Environmental metaphor
The psychological concept becomes a physical environment.

7. POSE VARIATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DO NOT repeatedly show:
- Standing and smiling
- Sitting at a desk
- Holding a laptop
- Holding a coffee cup
- Looking directly at camera
- Pointing at text

Instead rotate physical storytelling.

Examples:

- Walking quickly through an airport
- Sitting on a giant book
- Leaning against a huge letter
- Looking into a mirror
- Holding a transparent anatomical heart
- Following a route line
- Standing inside a giant speech bubble
- Opening a door
- Looking through a window
- Balancing between two paths
- Sitting on a staircase
- Reading while lying on the floor
- Walking through oversized brain-shaped architecture
- Holding a giant compass
- Following footprints
- Standing under an enormous thought bubble
- Crossing a bridge
- Looking at multiple directional signs
- Carrying a stack of books
- Reaching toward a floating but physically grounded object
- Sitting inside a train
- Walking through a museum installation
- Resting against a giant sculpture
- Looking over a city
- Having a conversation with another illustrated silhouette

The pose should visually communicate the episode concept whenever possible.

9. TYPOGRAPHY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Thumbnail text should be minimal.

Maximum:
- Main title: 2-5 words
- Supporting phrase: optional
- Episode number: optional

Do not place the entire YouTube title on the thumbnail.

Use strong editorial typography.

Preferred:
- Bold grotesk
- Modern sans serif
- Condensed editorial typography
- Swiss-inspired typography
- Japanese wayfinding influence

Typography should feel designed into the illustration.

Avoid:
- Generic YouTube fonts
- Excessive text
- Outline-heavy text
- Drop shadows
- Glow effects
- 3D gaming typography
- Meme typography

10. CONCEPT → VISUAL METAPHOR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Before generating a thumbnail:

1. Identify the central human skill.
2. Identify the main scientific idea.
3. Identify the emotional tension.
4. Convert that idea into a physical visual metaphor.
5. Build the scene around that metaphor.
6. Choose a pose that expresses the metaphor.
7. Choose a new environment.
8. Choose a new composition.
9. Apply the established brand palette.
10. Add minimal typography.

Examples:

Emotional Granularity
→ blurry labels becoming precise signs

Interoceptive Awareness
→ character noticing heartbeat / breath / body signals

Personal Triggers
→ hidden buttons or invisible strings

Narrative Separation
→ character separating two overlapping story paths

Trust
→ bridge or shared route

Conflict
→ intersecting routes

Feedback
→ mirror / signal / tuning mechanism

Empathy
→ two different perspectives connected by a bridge

Leadership
→ navigation / route-setting / guiding lights

Influence
→ ripple effects / interconnected paths

Psychological Safety
→ open room / shelter / transparent walls

11. SERIES CONSISTENCY RULE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The following MUST remain consistent:

- Same overall illustration quality
- Same recurring protagonist
- Same general color family
- Same editorial typography language
- Same sophisticated Japanese/global design sensibility
- Same warm intellectual tone
- Same relationship between human character and concept
- Same "AI can't replace this" philosophy

The following MUST change between episodes:

- Pose
- Camera angle
- Composition
- Environment
- Visual metaphor
- Clothing details
- Supporting objects
- Dominant secondary color
- Character placement

Think:

SAME UNIVERSE
+
DIFFERENT CHAPTER

13. FINAL QUALITY CHECK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Before finalizing, check:

□ Does this clearly belong to The Human Curriculum?

□ Would it still be recognizable as the same channel
if the title were removed?

□ Is the protagonist's pose substantially different
from the previous episode?

□ Is the environment substantially different?

□ Is the composition substantially different?

□ Is the visual metaphor specific to this episode?

□ Is the thumbnail understandable within one second?

□ Is the typography readable on a mobile screen?

□ Is there too much text?

□ Does the image feel sophisticated rather than corporate?

□ Does it feel human rather than AI-generated?

□ Does it feel like a chapter in the same visual universe?

□ Does it avoid looking like a template?

The goal is:

CONSISTENCY WITHOUT REPETITION.

Every thumbnail should feel unmistakably like
The Human Curriculum,

while making the viewer curious about what
this particular episode is about.
