DANIEL: The first one isn't about work directly, but I think it explains a lot. In 1998, Thomas Gilovich, Kenneth Savitsky, and Victoria Husted Medvec published a paper in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology about something they called "the illusion of transparency." The idea is that we overestimate how much other people can see what's going on inside us.
SENA: Inside us, like our feelings?
DANIEL: Yes. In one experiment, students at Cornell answered questions in front of a small group. Some were secretly told to lie.
