# The Human Curriculum — Thumbnail System v2 (October 2026)

Replaces "Rule.txt" and "Additional rule (14. Recurring Character Identity Lock)".
Use: paste this whole file into ChatGPT once, then send the episode brief (section 12). Attach the two most recent thumbnails so the style and the characters stay the same.

---

## 1. Brand core

Channel: The Human Curriculum (@learnwhataicant) — learn the human skills AI can't replace: emotional intelligence, trust, feedback, influence, speaking up, leadership, global workplace English.

Audience: mostly people outside Japan who work (or want to work) in global teams and are improving their English; also Japanese English learners.

The look: Japanese editorial magazine × Tokyo design museum × airport wayfinding × illustrated learning journal. Intelligent, warm, curious, modern. Never a generic productivity, business or self-help channel.

Goal: CONSISTENCY WITHOUT REPETITION. Same universe, different chapter.

---

## 2. Titles (how the thumbnail relates to the title)

- The YouTube title is written in plain words, from the viewer's problem, so people find it in search. Example: "Why Nobody Speaks Up in Your Meetings | English Podcast EP14". It is set automatically; it is NOT written on the thumbnail.
- The thumbnail text must ADD something the title doesn't say: the tension, a striking number, or the one memorable phrase of the episode.
  - Title "Why Nobody Speaks Up in Your Meetings" → thumbnail "10 Seconds of Silence"
  - Title "How to Start a Hard Conversation at Work" → thumbnail "How Are You, Really?"
- No clickbait (THIS WILL CHANGE YOUR LIFE, 5 SECRETS, scientists are shocked). Intelligent, not sensational.

---

## 3. Illustration style (fixed)

Sophisticated Japanese editorial magazine illustration:
- Flat, simplified shapes with hand-painted / gouache / risograph-like texture and slight paper grain
- Faces stylized and simple (small eyes, minimal lines), expressive through posture and gesture
- Limited palette, generous negative space, clear silhouettes that read at small size
- Feels like a cover or feature illustration in a premium magazine

Avoid: anime or manga styling (big shiny eyes, glossy hair strands, sparkles, blush stickers), chibi, cartoon mascots, photorealism, 3D renders, generic AI beauty, fashion-model perfection, corporate stock illustration, gradients and glow.

---

## 4. Characters — "same person, different day"

Keep each character's identity; change pose, outfit details, setting and mood.

**Sena (protagonist, in every thumbnail)** — Japanese woman in her late 20s, data analyst in Tokyo. Thoughtful, curious, quietly ambitious.
Signature items (keep all, every episode):
1. Long, wavy, light-brown hair (same silhouette)
2. Sunglasses pushed up on her head
3. Small gold hoop earrings
4. One dusty-pink piece of clothing (sweater, coat, scarf, shirt — it changes, the pink stays)
Everything else may change: outfit, bag, shoes, hair movement, expression.

**Daniel (only when the scene needs him)** — American man, late 30s, Seattle People Manager. Curly brown hair, light-blue shirt with rolled sleeves, company lanyard, navy mug. Sometimes with Maple, his golden retriever with a navy bandana.

**Coworkers (only when the episode is about them; simplified, never the focus)**
- Ryo — late 20s, short black hair, round glasses, navy cardigan, lanyard; kind and tired lately
- Kenta — early 30s, neat short black hair, plain grey knit, quiet and watchful
- Kato-san — 40s manager, short greying hair, white shirt, can of coffee
- Anyone else: a simple silhouette or seen from behind, no detailed face

---

## 5. Layout (fixed, so the channel is recognizable in a grid)

- Left 40%: text panel on cream torn paper. Right 60%: the illustration (it may bleed behind the panel).
- Top of the text panel: a small coral tag "EP 16".
- Main text: 2–5 words, very large, navy, bold condensed sans-serif (editorial / Swiss / wayfinding feel). Optional: one key word in coral.
- Optional second line: 2–4 words, smaller, navy.
- Nothing else is written anywhere. No channel name, no sticky notes, no handwritten notes, no book spines, no screens with text, no signs with readable words. (At phone size these become unreadable noise.) Exception: ONE object may carry 1–2 large words if that object IS the visual metaphor.
- Text must be readable when the thumbnail is 246 × 138 px (the size in YouTube's mobile feed).
- No outlines, drop shadows, glow, 3D or meme fonts.

---

## 6. Colors

Foundation: navy, sky blue, cream, soft white. Accents: coral, dusty pink, warm yellow/mustard. Occasional: green, burgundy.
Not every color every time; each episode has its own dominant accent. The series must still look like one family in a grid.

---

## 7. Composition rotation

Never repeat the composition of the previous episode, and avoid any used in the last 3 episodes. Record the letter in episodes.json as "thumb_comp".

A. Facing the viewer (not just smiling — doing something)
B. Walking through an illustrated environment
C. Sitting as part of an action (stairs, train, edge of a fountain) — never at a desk
D. Leaning on / pushing a large object
E. Looking up at a huge concept or shape
F. Seen from behind, entering a new place
G. Profile, watching something outside the frame
H. Small figure, huge environment
I. Close-up, face and shoulders, strong emotion
J. Wide scene, character small
K. Strong diagonal movement
L. Split scene: two places or two emotional states
M. Overhead view
N. Through a door or window
O. Transit: airport, station, route map
P. Holding, carrying, opening an object
Q. The concept becomes the environment

---

## 8. Poses, places, metaphors

Do not repeat: standing and smiling, sitting at a desk, typing on a laptop, holding a coffee cup, looking straight at the camera, pointing at text, video-call layouts.

Prefer the body telling the story: walking fast through a station, opening a door, crossing a bridge, balancing between two paths, carrying a stack of something, reaching for a floating object, following a route line, climbing stairs, looking through a window, standing inside a giant speech bubble.

Rotate places (not the same one twice in 3 episodes): Tokyo side street, train platform, airport, library, museum, rooftop, rainy crossing, garden, waterfront, Seattle hill, office corridor, hotel lobby, exhibition space, abstract psychological landscape.

Concept → metaphor examples: emotions → blurry labels becoming clear signs · body signals → heartbeat / breath as light · trust → bridge, shared path · conflict → crossing routes · feedback → mirror, tuning dial · psychological safety → open room, transparent walls · influence → ripples · leadership → route-setting, guiding lights · mistakes → a dropped object being picked up in daylight · stress → weather / pressure gauge.

---

## 9. Process for each episode

1. Core skill, main research idea, the episode's emotional tension.
2. Turn the tension into one physical metaphor.
3. Pick a composition (section 7) and a place (section 8) not used recently.
4. Pick a pose where Sena DOES something with the metaphor.
5. Choose the dominant accent color.
6. Write the thumbnail text (section 2 and 5).

---

## 10. Quality check

- Recognizable as The Human Curriculum even without the text?
- Is it Sena (all four signature items)?
- Magazine illustration, not anime?
- Composition, place, pose and metaphor different from the last episodes?
- Readable in one second at 246 × 138 px?
- Only the EP tag + main text (+ optional short line)? No small text anywhere?
- Human and specific, not stock or "AI-looking"?
